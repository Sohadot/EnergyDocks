Working definition

An energy dock is the physical and digital interface through which a mobile system connects to an energy source, establishes identity, transfers power and returns to operation.

01 / Contexts

One sequence. Three operating worlds.

Different machines arrive with different requirements. Each still needs a governed point of connection between mobility and energy.

  1. 01

    Marine

    Shore connection, port electrification, and autonomous surface or subsea systems returning to a docking point.

  2. 02

    Robotic

    Drones and field or warehouse robots that dock, charge and resume work without a person in the loop.

  3. 03

    Fleet

    Depot and on-route energy interfaces for buses, heavy trucks, port vehicles and yard equipment.

02 / Logic

The operating sequence.

The dock is more than the plug. It coordinates arrival, connection, identity, controlled transfer and release.

  1. 01

    Approach

    Locate the interface, enter its operating envelope and align.

  2. 02

    Connect

    Establish the physical, inductive or automated link.

  3. 03

    Identify

    Recognize the asset, session, permissions and demand.

  4. 04

    Transfer

    Control the flow of energy, measurement and safety state.

  5. 05

    Return

    Release the connection and return the system to service.

03 / The name

A name for the infrastructure layer.

EnergyDocks.com names the shared connection layer across marine, robotic and fleet systems—the point where mobility becomes energy infrastructure. Its plural form can hold a network of sites, a product family or a category, not merely a single charging device.
